Amateur radio explained
Voice, Morse, digital

And how does it work?

By voice, in Morse code or by computer – three of many modes. That variety is what makes amateur radio special.

Three of many ways
… and many more: pictures (SSTV), teletype (RTTY), television (ATV)
Voice
SSB · FM
What it sounds like
“CQ, CQ, this is DM6AX!”
On shortwave, your voice reaches across continents
On VHF/UHF loud and clear, more for the local area
Real conversations – like a phone call, no network
Good for: chatting – and listening in right here in the park
Morse
CW · telegraphy
What it sounds like
CQ CQ DE DM6AX K
Just tones: short and long, dot and dash
Gets around the world on very little power
Abbreviations hams understand worldwide
Good for: light packs and long distances
Digital
FT8 & co.
What it sounds like
CQ DM6AX JO40
The laptop sends and decodes automatically
Works even when the signal is barely audible
Short and sweet: callsign, location, signal report
Good for: tough conditions and lots of contacts
Good to know“CQ” means something like “Hello, can anyone hear me?”. And Morse is optional – in most countries you don’t need it for the exam.
